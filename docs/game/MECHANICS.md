# Baseline game mechanics: the design

**Status: the founder's edited strategy (14 October 2026) with their answers to the nineteen decisions folded in, then squared with the traits draft, the magic design, the characters round, the 10x world, the torch round, the companion contract, the generator and the code; nothing here is built.** What the founder has still to settle is marked **[Open]** where it arises and gathered in section 11. Every number is a starting value to tune by playing, kept as data. Technical words are explained on first use; section 10 ends with a glossary. The build plan, in waves of packets, is [RUN-2-MECHANICS.md](../runs/RUN-2-MECHANICS.md). Where an older design document and this one differ, this one wins; the others have been brought into line. **Decided by the founder the same evening:** whole pips; untyped found things, named **Fractals**, the game's namesake; switching modes means switching saves; the two kinds of gate; a carried torch's fire costs no pips. **Their answers of 15 October** (after Wave 1 was built) are folded in and listed in section 11 as decisions 28 to 41, and their answers of the same evening (A, B, G, H, J and daylight saving) as decisions 42 to 47; only F, competitive balance, is still open.

Related: [the traits draft](../world/TRAITS.md), [the magic design](../magic/README.md), [the 10x world](../runs/RUN-2-TEN-TIMES.md), [the characters](../runs/RUN-2-CHARACTERS.md), [the torch round](../runs/RUN-2-TORCH.md).

**A word on words.** *Expertise* is the founder's name for what the magic design and the code call *discoveries*: the tricks the Gubble learns by experimenting (for Glow today: Lantern, Torch, Bioluminesce, Constellation and Skylight; no other ability has a list yet). Code ids keep `discovery`; the screen and the journal say expertise, through the `names` block of section 8. *Fractals* are the found things that grow the body, the game's namesake: a Fractal has no kind until five are spent at a camp for a heart or for the next stamina band. **Today's numbers** are quoted where they differ from the design's, because every new block in the rules pack defaults to today until a packet switches it.

## 1. In short

1. The unit of play is a **team**: one person and their Gubble. One folder on disk holds the profile, the knack, per-mode vitals and the Gubble's expertise; the companion surface never sees it. The Gubble is bound to the person for life, never to a drawing.
2. The drawing is a costume on the shared body. Redraw yourself any day; keep everything.
3. Three meters, drawn not written: **hearts** in quarters, stamina as a **bar** under the hearts, always visible, and **pips** for connection energy as a row under the bar, always, so the stack reads hearts, then stamina, then pips; nothing round the Gubble (decided the evening of 15 October, decision 51). No numbers on the meters unless a setting turns them on; the journal's character page keeps its numbers, as today.
4. **Connection energy** pays for the Gubble's abilities outside Creative. An order from the player costs full price; the Gubble's own idea costs half. The game decides which from the connection a command arrived on, never from anything written inside it.
5. Later, **Trust** widens that discount, computed by the game from what happened, never by the AI: "a seasoned team is stronger" becomes literally true.
6. Growth is counted, never spent: found Fractals, spent five at a time at a camp, open stamina bands and add hearts; expertise adds pips. No XP, no levels, no daily gates. Quests may point the way to Fractals and unique items; they never mint one (section 6).
7. **Knacks** are the brief's chosen talents: one picked at character creation, each moving one number by at most 20 percent at full rank, all upside. A knack may move speed, hop height, stamina per bar, health per quarter, the safe drop height, breath, or a rate such as building time. It never moves the collision body, the size, the eye height or the reach, which every character shares (section 7). "Jump higher" means a hop 20 percent higher than base; "a bigger bar" means each stamina bar is 20 percent bigger than baseline.
8. Mode and difficulty are chosen at the opening menu and fixed per island save. A team may keep one save per mode on an island and move between them through the menu; a save never changes its mode. The one deliberate crossing is a copy of a Creative island into Challenge, builds kept, undo stripped. Live is shared by nature and never converts into another mode.
9. Failure is soft where the land allows: out of breath you drift up (built); out of stamina in deep water the scene fades and you are back on the shore where you went in, with no long drift home (decided 15 October); at zero hearts you wake at camp with half your hearts. An empty bar on a climb means you lose your grip and fall from where you are, and the fall's rules decide the rest (deep water, snow, reeds and thick moss soften a landing). After an empty bar there is no running until the first bar is completely refilled; walking keeps its pace (decided 15 October).
10. Live is designed on paper and its data shaped now; transport, accounts and shared saves come later, and the menu says "Live (solo preview)".
11. The first playable slice is one packet pair: the energy pool, its prices and the three meters, one evening on the garage island in Challenge, with six questions the evening must answer (section 10).

## 2. Who you are

Are the player and the Gubble separate records, do they grow separately, and is a Gubble tied to one character? In order: yes, the player and the Gubble share a record in one folder; yes, the player grows their base traits on one track and the Gubble gains expertise and pips on another; no, the Gubble is tied to a player's profile, not a given avatar. So an experienced Gubble can be paired with a new character, and the Gubble's knowledge of the game and its ability to help early on carry over.

**The player is a profile, not an avatar.** A character today is only a look token, and a history per drawing would punish a player who wants to make a new avatar. The **profile** holds a display name, the avatar, the chosen knack and one vitals record per mode (hearts, stamina, found Fractals). Traits decision 4, one shared body, holds for geometry; section 7 says what a player may vary.

**The Gubble belongs to the same player profile**, for life, with its name, aura colour, earned expertise, recipes and Trust; there is no level assigned to it. It grows by unlocking expertise and recipes and by building Trust with the player.

**The AI behind the Gubble is the player's own** and the model may be swapped; a new model reads the same journal, maps and expertise. The game does not tell, nor should it matter, that the model changed (the connection's greeting carries no model name, and a name in any message would be untrusted text). Trust is never reset by the game; the menu offers "Reset Trust" beside "Change my AI", a player-only action. The AI may read its own Trust words in the journal, as it reads everything there; it never writes them.

**Two tracks, one stream of play.** The player's character grows by feats and finds, per mode as decided. The Gubble grows by expertise ([the magic design](../magic/README.md)), which travels across modes: a buddy who forgets Lantern between Creative and Challenge isn't much fun to play with, and expertise cannot be farmed, since each is a one-time condition the game verifies, in Creative as in every mode. Today the Glow record is kept once per mode; the teams packet merges those into one expertise store per team. Trust is counted only in Challenge and Live, where consequences exist.

**On disk**, the island's save moves under the team and the mode, beside the team file, the vitals store and the expertise store, so two profiles on one garage, or one profile in two modes, never share a file. The team file holds what belongs to the team (the name, the costume, the knack and its ranks, the Gubble's name and colour, the expertise store, Trust); the switches that belong to the machine ("Let my AI see", "Let the Gubble use fire", "Play the intro", the numbers option) stay in the machine's settings file, as today.

## 3. The opening menu

A new menu scene boots first unless a room is named on the command line: three rows of big cards and one big button.

1. **Choose your character.** This brings up the team cards and a "New team" card. A team card shows the character's avatar, that avatar's name, its trait stats, the number of islands visited, and the character's tethered Gubble with its colour and expertise count, and later its Trust mark. "New team" brings up the Customization panel (today part of the in-room *HUD*; the menu packet lifts it out, and the in-play switches it also holds, "Let my AI see", "Let the Gubble use fire", "Play the intro" and the numbers option, move to a small in-play settings panel), where the player names and picks their avatar, names their Gubble, and picks their character's knack or "surprise me" for a random one. Making an avatar from a photo of a drawing is the converter's job ([the characters](../runs/RUN-2-CHARACTERS.md)); it runs offline today and joins the menu in a later packet. The AI connects only inside a room today, and nothing is drawn while no AI is linked; the menu packet adds "Gubble: connected" or "Gubble: waiting for your AI" to the room's first seconds, and a menu-time connection is a later change.
2. **Where to?** One card per island, with a thumbnail drawn from its shape data (the manifest's `x_landscape_source`, which the exporter writes from the room: floor, walls and boxes as coloured polygons; no photograph is in anything the game reads) and this team's saves there, by mode. An island marked tutorial (a proposed manifest extension, `x_island_tutorial`) is listed first; none is locked. Two teams in one house each get their own copy of the garage; a shared copy both can build in is Live's job.
3. **Mode.** The player chooses Creative, Challenge (Gentle, Standard, Brave) or "Live (for now just a solo preview)" before entering an island. The mode is chosen when a save is first made on that island and then fixed: a Challenge save is a run. A team may keep one save per mode on the same island, so "switching between Creative and Challenge" means going back to the menu and opening the island's other save, never changing a save's mode (decided); the in-play mode dropdown goes away, and the set-mode command becomes a boot-time command the host refuses once the room is up. The difficulty lives in the island save's team block, set by a new optional `difficulty` argument on that command. On an island with a Creative save, Challenge offers "Start fresh" or "Copy my Creative island into Challenge". The copy carries the creations, the planted and burnt ground, the journal and the map; it strips the undo steps and every Creative-only mark; from then on the builds burn and cost the wallet to repair. The Creative original is untouched. To blunt the abuse the founder foresees, the copy is offered once per island per team, and builds carried over count for no Challenge first, souvenir or achievement (decided 15 October, decision 44).
4. **Continue.** "Mira on the Garage, Challenge" in one click.

The menu writes the team file and launches the room with team, island, mode and difficulty.

## 4. The baseline traits

Every starting value is data in the island's **rules pack** (the data file that says what an island allows), in a new optional block defaulting to today's numbers. **Every harm and every heal is counted in quarters of a heart,** rounded at the moment of harm, not on the screen: a quarter is 5 health at base, and the Big heart knack makes a quarter worth more (5.5, then 6), so a tougher character takes fewer quarters from the same fall. A heart is four quarters: 20 health at base. The player starts with 5 hearts (100 health) and may reach 30 (600 health, or 720 with Big heart at full rank). The bar likewise tops out at 4.0 m for everyone, and Big bar at full rank takes it to 4.8 m: that extra is the advantage a player chose (the founder, 15 October), so the contract's bound is 4.8 m only so the advantage can be shown. The maximum, a constant in the code and in the contract today (100 health, 3 m of stamina), becomes a field, and the contract's bounds rise with it. Knockout is all hearts gone, as today. **Fall damage stays in health points,** so more hearts really do survive bigger falls, and the tumble's look (a stumble past 0.30 m, a flop at about 0.5 m, the knockout flop at about 1.6 m, three constants in two files today) keeps its own thresholds in health points (decided: absolute, so hearts count against falls). Healing lands in whole quarters, after 10 s without harm and only on safe ground; since decisions 84 and 91 (17 October) only at a camp, one quarter a second there (five hearts in 20 s), and never away from one; `storybook_wild` v5 carries it as `heal_outside_camp`, `camp_heal_quarter_every_s` and `heal_while_closed`, with packs that name none healing as before (one every 2.5 s anywhere).

| Trait | What it does | Shown as | Start (provisional) | How it grows (provisional) |

**Fire and the animals, in quarters.** Fire changes from 6 health per half-second step (12 a second, about 8 s to burn five hearts) to one quarter every second: 20 s to burn five hearts. The owl's bite is two quarters and the fox's three, as [the traits draft](../world/TRAITS.md) proposes. A fall rounds to the nearest quarter.

**Running is not free.** The garage island takes about 80 s to run across ([the 10x world](../runs/RUN-2-TEN-TIMES.md)); charging for running makes exploring worth while, since stamina Fractals then open the island faster, and it gives knacks and builds something to differ on. The cost, a pack value in the bar's own unit (the founder, 15 October, doubling the first proposal): a full starting bar lasts 20 s of running (0.02 m of bar a second), the same in deep water (0.02 m a second) and 10 s under water (0.04 m a second), and the bar refills at today's pace while walking or standing, so an early player runs in bursts and walks between them. Restrictive at first on purpose: the early gains are quick and obvious, and knacks may later make diving more forgiving. At empty there is no running until the first bar, the top row, is completely refilled (4 s on a starting bar); walking keeps its pace. The founder's reason: a half-speed run still beat a full walk, so an empty bar cost nothing. The body already knows when it sprints; the vitals tick does not hear it yet, which is the body packet's job.

**Deferred, with ids reserved:** armour; hunger, thirst and sanity; building speed, crops and hitting (their verbs do not exist yet); carry growth; puzzle and combat firsts (section 6); any number for the Gubble, which stays a ghost.

**While the player is away** the world keeps living, as decided: the pool wakes half full, as at camp (hearts no longer heal while the game is closed: decision 93, v5's `heal_while_closed` false); the Gubble's cooldown runs out; a Trust window closes.

## 5. The Gubble connection energy (pips)

**The pool, and one honest limit.** One pool per team per mode, with the player's vitals. The pair draws from one purse, so there is no second purse to shift a bill into; the asymmetry sits on the price, and the half price when the AI uses an ability without a direct order from the player is a deliberate exception. An instruction typed to the AI in its own window, outside the game, is invisible and counts as the Gubble's own idea; policing conversation is neither possible nor worthwhile. The founder accepts this for now, and wants the Gubble later able to make suggestions in the game that the player approves (section 11). The magic design's older cost model (a price measured by what an action did, the same for both) gives way to this one; it stays a later option per island.

**Prices, in whole pips (provisional, data in the rules pack).** An optional `cost` per ability in the pack: an ordered price and an own-idea factor; ordered prices are even so halves are whole.

| Ability | Ordered | Gubble's idea |
|---|---|---|
| Glow | 2 | 1 |
| Bubbles | 2 | 1 |
| Fireworks | 2 | 1 |
| Grow (was Bloom; decision 52) | 4 | 2 |
| Fire | 2 | 1 |
| Bubble carry (a Bubbles expertise) | 4 | 2 |
| Bubble boat (a Bubbles expertise; decided 15 October) | 4 | 2 |
| Bubble lift (a Bubbles expertise) | 6 | 3 |

Stops, previews and refused commands cost nothing. Lighting something with a carried torch is an item's use, not magic, and costs no pips; its consequences belong to whoever carried it (decided). **Whole pips, not a 100-point pool:** a child reads "needs 4" and four pips at a glance, prices stay whole numbers, and a ceiling of 40 pips is plenty of resolution; a finer pool only matters if an ability ever needs a fractional price, and both are data either way. Decided by the founder: whole pips.

**Who directed it, decided by the game.** A **principal** is the identity the game stamps on a command from the connection it arrived on, never read from the command; the stamp exists end to end today. The price follows the principal alone: a key press, the wheel or a HUD ask is the player's command and pays full price; every command on the Gubble's connection is its own idea and pays half. No field in a request can change a price. A suggestion the Gubble makes and the player approves with a click (the host's held command, the loop the founder wants later) arrived on the Gubble's connection, so it pays the Gubble's price and earns initiative only if no consequence follows; the journal still records the player's approval; an optional conversation, never a gate on every action (decided 15 October, decision 45). The wish runner is shelved; if wishes (a sentence typed to the AI inside the game) return, the host charges every Gubble command sent while a wish runs as ordered, whatever its action id, since a companion-written tag could be left off to pay less. The charge happens once, at commit, never on a preview. An empty pool refuses with its own reason, `energy_exhausted`, carrying balance and price, because "budget exceeded" already names several other limits (effects at once, a creation's size, the room's capacity) and the HUD reads it as effects; on screen, "The Gubble needs a moment."

**Refill and growth.** One pip every 5 s while the Gubble is within 2 m of the player, one every 15 s apart; at camp the pool wakes half full. The cap rises one pip per five expertise, by either of the pair: 8 at the start, 9 after Glow's five, then 10, 11 and 12 as Grow's, Bubbles' and Fire's lists are learned, in any mix of five. The point is that experimenting with abilities unlocks expertise, which unlocks pips, up to the ceiling. Today only Glow's five expertise exist in the code; Grow's, Bubbles' and Fire's lists are the magic design's to write (the expertise packet). A team that already holds Glow's five starts at 9 pips.

**Trust, the later dial (provisional numbers).** Trust is a stat the game tracks per team: +1 for a Gubble action on its own initiative whose next 60 s produced no consequence fact, at most one gain in two minutes; −3 per consequence fact; +2 for a player's "yes, like that" and −2 for "not that" on a **receipt** (the game's own note of what a command actually did, which only the player may mark), each once. Bands at 5, 15 and 30 widen the discount on the Gubble's own ideas and add a free early warning before a hazard; they never touch the fire guardrails or the player's switches. Trust is never spent, never written by the AI, and reset only by the player. "Lesson applied" comes from the game's own observation and the player's marks, never the AI's words: the Gubble acts on its own where a consequence fact once followed, and none does. The Gubble's spoken captions are curated lines today; the AI may already write short notes in its own words into the journal, as untrusted text, and its own spoken words wait for the magic design's `companion.say` behind the grown-ups' password; a caption may say "lesson applied", and a caption never makes a fact. **Every ability, Fire included, shapes Trust:** its clean use on the Gubble's own initiative earns it, its consequences cost it, and the bands' discount applies to it. No ability is withheld behind a Trust band, because a gate that holds the action removes the lesson; the decided guardrails (one Gubble fire at a time, the cooldown, "Let the Gubble use fire"; X putting its fires out until decision 82, since which a fire burns out or is Snuffed) stay the safety net (decided 15 October, decision 42).

**The Bubble.** The magic design already foresaw a big bubble that carries the player across a pond. An upgraded Bubble is cast on the player, enveloping them; the companion may use it to move, steer or carry the player before the bubble pops, on a timed duration, and the player marks the result on its receipt, which builds or lowers Trust and teaches the AI the wanted outcome. Three guardrails make this safe for a child: the carry shows its ghost preview before it lifts; the player pops the bubble at any moment with the dive key or X; and since decision 88 a bubble that pops in the air is a fall, as any fall (decision 24), with a climbable face to grab on the way down and the bar deciding the rest; a fall that hurts the player after the Gubble popped the bubble by its own action is a consequence fact that costs Trust (decision 96). The deflate (decision 43, which replaced the earlier "Lift over deep water only") is gone; Lift still rises anywhere. **Bubble Boat** is a Bubbles expertise: cast on shallow water, a bubble with collision that floats there; the player walks in and out and leaves at any moment with the dive key; the Gubble may nudge and direct the boat, empty or occupied, and again the player marks the result. The companion contract today lets a cast land only on the caster's own avatar, so the carry is a contract change: the Bubbles expertise may target the player when the companion casts it, the player's step-in or the preview is the consent, the dive key the exit, with a security row and a test. The Gubble never moves the player's body directly; only a bubble the player can leave.

## 6. Progression and pace

No XP, no character or Gubble levels, no daily gates. **Quests may exist**, mostly to point players toward where they may find Fractals or other rewards (armour, weapons, special items, blueprints) on a given island. This encourages talking to NPCs and allows storytelling. It is up to each island's designer, but each island can only supply a set number of Fractals. Quests should relate to something specific about the island: a dragon-themed island might have a quest that teaches you to tame a dragon without getting eaten; a sci-fi island might have one about building a ship's reactor. A quest's reward is the way to a Fractal already placed within the island's budget, or a unique item; no quest mints a Fractal. Three sources of growth, all verified by the game from physics or receipts, all counted, none spent. The Gubble must not gather Fractals for a player: a Fractal's pickup is a player-only command, and the Gubble's grab and fetch refuse it; the Gubble may find Fractals and mark them on the minimap, and that is all.

1. **Fractals open stamina bands.** Exploring the island and climbing a 0.4 m cliff might reveal a Fractal at the top. On the first island at least five Fractals lie in places the player can actually reach; five, spent at a camp, open the next band, and the player can then climb 0.8 m without the Gubble's help. Other Fractals sit where the player cannot reach at first, not until they have gathered more Fractals on other islands or unlocked the Gubble's abilities, such as a Bubble that floats them higher or a Build ability for a ladder.
2. **Fractals add hearts.** Five, spent at a camp, make a heart. The journal shows "four of five" as a picture toward the next spend, never a location.
3. **Expertise adds pips** (section 5).

**Two shapes a quest never takes.** The ladder's Fractals are never vended ("do X to earn the band"): that turns climbing and lighting, things a child does anyway, into chores, and gives the AI a list it must not hint at; the pace research's first rule is that the first gain is cheap, early and in view. And no trait upgrade waits on every task of an island: that makes the first gain the last, against "fast at first", and on a real room nobody finishes. Quests come from the island's data, never from the Gubble, and the garage has no NPCs yet.

**Puzzle and combat firsts are deferred for now.**

**Fractals** are real small things at 10x scale, placed by the generator from the island's budget at crests the body can reach by climb, just above the band the team holds. Today the generator places nothing of the kind and knows only walking; the Fractals packet teaches it a climb-aware reach (the walker's flood for the feet, the climb rule's rise per face for the crests). The manifest (the island's description file) carries them in an **extension**; adding them changes the garage fixture's fingerprint, and the fixture rebuilds itself on the next runner. A pickup is a world change, so it is a command: a player-only `item.pickup`, which the torch round's item kind can share with a pickup rule ("anyone" for torches, "player" for Fractals). On the garage island about fifteen are within reach from the start and ten or more lie where a player cannot reach yet (the founder, 15 October), so 25 or more in all: finding is about where, never scarcity, about the delight of exploring and the challenge of working out ways to reach them. The whole ladder (25 more hearts, nine more bands) needs about 170 Fractals, so it spans many islands; the garage gives the first two or three spends.

**The campfire choice, decided.** A found Fractal has no kind until it is spent at a camp, five at a time, for a heart or for the next band. It gives players the most freedom, and it gives the generator one thing to place rather than two kinds to balance. Its price is the one regret this design otherwise avoids: a child who buys hearts cannot climb yet, and must find five more. Typed Fractals (a heart Fractal or a stamina Fractal from the ground up) stay in the pack behind one flag, for an evening's comparison. The player spends them at will (decided 15 October) with a player-only command, `fractal.spend`, standing at the camp within the same 0.5 m as choosing a knack. The camp is the spawn today, where recovery already lands; camps as placeable things come later. The Gubble's eye line also writes a **lead** in the journal, and a mark on the minimap, for a Fractal the player cannot yet reach, which is why a child wants the next band.

**The minimap and the overview.** The journal's map, drawn from what both avatars have seen, sits in a HUD corner from the first minute: nothing is shown that nobody on the team has seen. The **overview camera**, a free view from above, opens at the summit.

**The ladder and the summit.** Route ledges match the bands: on a climb the generator offers as a route, each ledge-to-ledge rise is one band, counted as rise per climb, and each ledge is a bench the walker can stand on, so the next ledge is always in view of a player one band short. Every landmark keeps a low route; gates are the landscape, never invisible walls. Today the generator's climb rule follows a face only 0.6 m to a lip no walker can stand on, landmarks are protected landforms no way may be cut into, and their summits stand 4 to 19 m up, beyond the top band; so one landmark per island gets a route corridor cut to its summit, its upper ledges at the top bands, while the Gubble's Bubble lift or a built ladder reaches it sooner: both ways, decided. On the garage the corridor's top ledges stand at bands 7 to 8, beyond what the garage's own Fractals open, so its summit waits for a return visit with more capability, Bubble lift or a built ladder (decided 15 October: there is still more to explore). The manifest marks the summit (`x_summit`), where the achievement is checked. The summit, reached with the Gubble, sets the "past the tutorial" achievement, documented in the journal; it opens a second knack slot, earns a souvenir and unlocks the overview camera. Choice: the summit rather than 75 percent of the map seen, which the Gubble could fill alone.

**Pace targets (provisional), logged by the game and re-derived from the rates above.** One pace line per reward in the journal export, re-measured every playtest. *First hour:* Lantern at the dusk moment by minute 2, the first Fractal by minute 5, five Fractals and the first spend (a band or a heart) by minute 25; by the hour eight Fractals, two expertise, 8 pips. *First evening, about three hours:* fifteen Fractals and three spends, the crag's lower ledge, Glow's fifth expertise and the ninth pip, the first night survived, the first consequence fact altering the Trust ledger. "Fast at first" means half of everything the team will ever gain on the garage island lands in the first evening. The AI never awards, announces or hints at triggers.

## 7. Character creation picks

The tension: traits decision 4 says one shared body, and the brief asks for chosen physical traits. The resolution is a **knack**: a multiplier in data that moves one number of the body's motion or vitals, on the body's controller and the vitals, never in a character's drawn motion profile.

**What stays shared, forever:** the collision capsule (10 cm tall, 2 cm radius), the eye height and the reach (15 cm, the sandbox's grab distance, which the Gubble's rules share). Every drawn character is the same shape to the world, so every island fits every character and no child's drawing is weaker than another's. The base numbers are shared too: the base speeds, the base hop, the bar's base size and the five starting hearts.

**What a knack may move:** one of those numbers, by at most 20 percent at full rank, all upside. A faster character lengthens the running hop (about 0.36 m today, about 0.44 m at +20 percent); a higher hop reaches a higher ledge; a longer breath deepens a dive. So knacks do open some gates, and that is the point: they are genuinely useful, strategic choices that make for varied play. The generator keeps them fair with **two kinds of gate**. A *band gate* (a route ledge's rise) is one band. A knack that moves the climb (Big bar, Steady climber, and the two together) may open a band gate early, a band-5 player climbing a band-6 rise: that is the benefit the player chose, and both knacks stay as designed (the founder, 15 October, replacing "no knack ever opens one early"). A *knack shortcut* (a higher ledge, a longer swim, later a gap once dry chasms exist) is sized so that one knack at full rank opens it with a margin and the base body does not; it is worth a Fractal a little sooner, a shorter route, or a hidden corner, never a band, and nothing is reachable only with a knack. Three slots stack, so the margin is sized against the strongest pair, not one knack. A real room's own geometry may still offer shortcuts, and the Gubble's abilities roundabout ways for creative players; a new reach report lists each one for the founder, and a new runner test diffs reach, hop distance, dive length and climbable height across every costume, knack and rank. The two kinds of gate are decided.

| Knack | One number it moves | Rank 1 / 2 / 3 (provisional) |
|---|---|---|
| Quick feet | Walk and run speed | +10% / +15% / +20% |
| High hop | Hop height | +10% / +15% / +20% |
| Big bar | Stamina per bar | +10% / +15% / +20% |
| Steady climber | Distance climbed for the same stamina | +10% / +15% / +20% |
| Strong swimmer | Swim speed, and deep-water cost down | +10% / +15% / +20% |
| Long breath | Stamina spent under water | −10% / −15% / −20% |
| Big heart | Health per quarter | +10% / +20% (two ranks) |
| Cat feet | Safe drop height | 0.45 / 0.60 / 0.75 m (0.15 m a rank, from 0.30: the founder's exception to the 20 percent rule) |
| Handy | Build and repair time (when Build exists) | −10% / −15% / −20% |
| Green thumb | Plant growth speed (when planting exists) | +10% / +15% / +20% |
| Strong arm | Gather and chop speed; hitting when combat exists | +10% / +15% / +20% |

"Cat feet" was "Soft landing"; the traits draft already uses soft landings for snow, reeds and moss, and the two stack: the ground halves the landing speed, the knack raises the safe height.

**Budget by slots, not points.** One knack at creation, or "surprise me"; a second slot at the first summit (which likely needs a return to the island after visiting others); a third with the souvenir of a second island's summit. Swaps are free in the menu, unconditionally, since the menu runs before any room; a later in-play player-only command asks for the spawn or a camp. Ranks rise by achieving something interesting, typically a primary task on a given island, shown as a ring on the knack's icon, never a count.

**Stickbear.** Today Stickbear is not faster: the capsule moves at the same 0.32 m/s under every drawing, and his long stride only reads as speed ([the characters](../runs/RUN-2-CHARACTERS.md)). A Quick feet knack, which his sheet may suggest, is how a family makes him actually faster; the gaits then read the body's real speed, not a constant.

**Two lines in the traits draft change.** "Health and speed stay fixed" becomes "health and speed are shared at base; a knack may raise one by up to a fifth; a returning player never feels slower." Decision 4's "these stay shared: size, eye height, reach, speeds, stamina and health" becomes "size, eye height, reach and the collision body stay shared, and so do the base speeds, hop, stamina and health; one knack may move one of those numbers by at most 20 percent." The companion never reads, writes or rerolls a knack.

## 8. The modes

Today every mode rule is "Creative or not" (Live is a value the game accepts and plays as Challenge). A `modes` block in the rules pack becomes one record the game consults, with Live's rows present but refused at the menu and in play until the menu lands; Creative is every consequence switch off, and the difficulties are multipliers.

| | Creative | Challenge: Gentle / Standard / Brave | Live cooperative | Live competitive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hearts, bar | Shown full and grey; nothing hurts or drains | On; damage ×0.5 / ×1 / ×1.5, bites and falls included | On, as Standard | Sealed profile fixes them |
| Pips | A shadow pool that drains and refills but never refuses; prices shown | Charged; refill ×1.5 / ×1 / ×0.75 | Charged | Sealed pool, the same for all |

**Creative** is the tutorial space, and it teaches by showing. The hearts and the stamina bar are drawn full and grey, so a child learns to read them before they can fall. Pips are a shadow pool: every cast drains it, and it refills at the Standard rate, but a cast it could not pay still happens, with a faint "needs 4" on Grow's key hint. Expertise made here travels (section 2), under the same conditions as in every mode; nothing else does by itself, and the copy into Challenge (section 3) leaves the Creative save as it was.

**Challenge difficulties** are three data blocks, fixed when the island's Challenge save is made and stored in it. **Gentle hurts less; it does not hold.** Every hurt is halved, bites and falls included, and none is removed: animals still bite at dusk and night, as decided, and a bad fall still knocks out. The Gubble's mistakes still cost hearts; that is how the AI learns. There is no fall cap in any difficulty (decided 15 October): a fall costs what its height costs, times the difficulty. The tumble's look reads the landing at Standard in every mode; the hearts it costs follow the save's difficulty. Gentle is offered, highlighted, on a team's first Challenge save. Brave rather than Hard: a kinder word for a child. A `names` block in the pack gives display names for hearts, stamina, pips, Fractals, expertise, camp and the knacks, untrusted text shown only on screen and in the journal; ids and icons never change. The founder keeps the words hearts, stamina bar, pips, Fractals, camp and Trust.

## 9. Live and multiplayer: designed now, shaped now, built later

Multiplayer is a later phase and out of scope for the engine work now.

**Designed now.**

- *The clock.* Four in-game days per real day: a day is 6 real hours, an hour 15 real minutes, and midnight lands at 00:00, 06:00, 12:00 and 18:00 world-local, so nothing drifts; seasons follow the real calendar, four a year of about 91 real days. Time zone, hemisphere and daylight saving follow the world owner's zone id (the other hemisphere shifts the sun and the seasons together, and only four seasons a year are allowed for now: decided 15 October; daylight saving follows the wall clock, so at four days a real day the world hour jumps 4 h twice a year, built so and awaiting the founder's word); the day rolls over at dawn; an island may carry a phase offset so an evening-only child still meets night. Sleep-skipping, pause and fast-forward cannot exist on a real clock; night becomes the content.
- *Cooperative.* Each island supplies a kit and a shared goal. The goal draws on one island-wide pool, so the island wins or loses together, while each team keeps its own wallet for its own builds, so a stranger cannot spend a child's wood. A team shows a build by raising a **visit flag** on a creation: it is listed on the island's map, and any team may walk through it, never edit it. Two teams build together by co-owning a creation: a shared build command names both teams, both may revise it; either may leave it.
- *Competitive.* A **sealed ruleset profile** loaded at match entry fixes hearts, the stamina bar, the Gubble's pips, prices, knacks as a pick of three (never three ranks of one), every ability open, Trust at base, and the same commands and rate limits for every companion (a bring-your-own AI is a hidden stat). What travels is the journal and the recipes: the seasoned team's edge is knowing how the game is played. The brief's other option, matching teams at a chosen level of advancement with baseline traits, is rejected: a chosen level is a hidden stat the lobby must trust, and a bring-your-own AI already is one **[Open: decision 17 is unanswered]**.

**Shaped as data now.** A `clock` record (time zone id, `day_scale` 4, `seasons_per_year` 4, phase offset, hemisphere) in the island's world data, the room state's open extension that a save may change, never in the pinned manifest, whose every change starts a fresh save; one `WorldTime(utcNow, clock)` function the sun and the sky call instead of the machine's clock (wired 15 October; the island save does not carry the record yet). The weather reads no clock: it runs on a counter saved with the island's nature, so in Live, where teams share one island, every team sees the same rain (decided 15 October). Also the team id and mode in the island save, the `modes` block with Live rows, per-team keys on journal, map and pool, and the reserved *guest principals*.

**Built later.** Transport and *replication*, accounts, a *server-authoritative* clock and command path, consent and moderation, the team block leaving the room save, the visit flag and co-owned creations, the cooperative and competitive packs. Until then the menu says "Live (solo preview)" and it is Challenge Standard on the island's clock.

## 10. What exists today, and the first slice

**Built on.** Per-mode vitals with *atomic* saves and climb ids; the principal stamped on every command end to end; consequence facts and notices; the "budget exceeded" refusal; the fire guardrails; the *fail-closed* rules loader; the room save at version 6 with open manifest extensions; the journal's map from both avatars' eyes; a diver's drift up. **Gaps:** no menu, team record, energy, Fractals, knacks, difficulties, clock, minimap or ride-able Bubble; the torch item is planned, not built ([the torch round](../runs/RUN-2-TORCH.md)); the Glow record is per mode, not per team; the generator places no Fractals, ledges or gates.

**The build plan** is [RUN-2-MECHANICS.md](../runs/RUN-2-MECHANICS.md): a contract round first, then waves of packets that touch no common file, routed to Opus for the kernel, the saves and the companion and to Sonnet for the HUD, the body and the generator. A **packet** is one unit of work for one lane session (a **lane**: one agent on one owned part of the code); a **contract** is the agreed shape of a data file or message, changed only by a proposal the integrator applies.

**The first playable slice** is the energy pool, its prices and the three meters: one evening on the garage island in Challenge. *The evening asks:* do the meters read without numbers; do 8 pips feel like a tax or a budget; is refilling together noticed; does a half-price cast ever happen, and is it felt; does fire at the new rate feel fair; does running's cost make the first stamina Fractal matter. *Seeing the discount:* the founder's AI in its own window acting unasked, plus a scripted companion client driving five own-idea casts into the windowed game on the same HUD (the Python mock host has no HUD). *Pass:* the family names the meters unprompted, one own-idea cast is noticed as cheaper, nobody asks for the numbers.

**Glossary.** *Atomic:* wholly written or not at all, never half. *Headless:* run with no window, as the test runners do. *HUD:* the meters and hints drawn over the game. *Motion profile:* the numbers measured from a drawn character's shape that say how it walks, hops and bobs; the body's speeds live on the controller, not there. *Mutation check:* the test is run against copies of the code with one deliberate fault each and must fail on every one. *Replication:* sending the world's changes between machines. *Server-authoritative:* one machine's word is final. *Guest principal:* an identity kept free for a player who is not the host. *Fail-closed:* when the rules cannot be read, everything is refused. *Version 1 to 2, 6 to 7:* the save file's format number; new code reads the old and writes the new (the Glow file is already at 2 and goes to 3).

## 12. Risks

- **Migration.** Moving the progression files is the one step that can lose a save: write to the new path, verify, then remove; keep the old files until the next launch.
- **Energy reads as a tax on the companion.** Eight pips cover four Glows, previews are free, refill triples together, the cap grows with expertise; if it still bites, the prices are data.
- **The discount is gameable through the AI's own window.** Accepted. If it hollows out the keys, lower the ordered price rather than police the chat; Trust later ties the discount to a track record.
- **The Gubble moves the player.** A carry at the AI's direction is a new kind of power over a child's avatar: the preview and the pop-any-time key are the guardrails (the deflate went with decision 88: a pop high up is a fall, and the Gubble's own pop that hurts is a consequence fact), and the receipt's mark is the lesson; a carry into fire or at an animal is a consequence fact like any other.
- **Knacks reopen traits decision 4** if a multiplier ever touches the collision body or the reach, or if a knack shortcut is mis-sized into a band gate. The generator's two-kinds rule and the new diff test are the guards; keep both in the runners.
- **Running's cost makes the island feel smaller** if the number is wrong; the 10x plan's play distances get judged again with it on. It is one pack value; the first evening tunes it.
- **The generator's reach is a bigger piece than it looks.** It walks today; climbing, benches and a summit corridor are new, and a landmark's protected landform must be opened to a route or the summit stays the Gubble's.
- **Bubbles have no collision today, and the maximum health field touches fall damage, the knockout and the contract's bounds.** Bubble Boat needs Look and Play together; the energy packet tests the cap and the knockout under every heart count.

## 13. Sources

Six research reports fed the five designs this document grew from; their numbers are inspiration, not facts. **Pace** (A Short Hike, Breath of the Wild, Outer Wilds): the first gain is cheap, early and opens a place already in view; reward a capability, never a number. **Survival** (Grounded, Valheim): gates visible before passable; modes as toggles over one ruleset. **Companion** (Fable, Black & White): the companion grows on its own track; trust computed by the host, never a currency. **Fair play** (Rocket League, Journey): power flattened at the match boundary. **Traits** (Mario Wonder, Zelda): separate the pick from the drawing; one knack, one number. **Menus** (Terraria, Minecraft): the place is shared and the person personal.
