# Look bible: storybook painterly

**Status: candidate (Run 1, Look lane).** The founder gave the look verdict on 7 October ("The captures are fine. No big changes necessary.") and locked v1 as the look-gate candidate. The founder's direction of 6 October: **style first, in the spirit of Tiny Glade; material media later**, and camera angles that make the scene read as artistic. The founder's **Look direction of 7 October** is folded in below: lived-in detail, colour contrast as a way to focus, season palettes, golden window light with warm-white accents, creamy bokeh, and an observe view with a very tight tilt-shift band. ## Style first, Tiny Glade spirit; media later

The founder's decision (6 October): **do not pick a first material medium yet.** The player will later choose a medium (felt, stone, clay, yarn, cardboard and so on) in which every surface manifests, and each medium will need research into real handcrafted work. That comes after one artistic style is settled.

The style comes first, and it is **one stylized storybook style in the spirit of Tiny Glade**: cozy and fanciful (the words the founder wants players to use), cottage-core, handmade, never photorealistic. Concretely:

- **Stylized, not photographic.** Painted rather than photographed surfaces, soft rounded forms, and every leaf, flower and shingle reading as a distinct object (Tiny Glade's procedural detail; L5 work for later runs).
- **Light that tells the hour.** Warm twilight and sunset pastels (orange, gold and light blue), deep blue nights with warm lamps, golden afternoons. Fairy lights and glowing windows give a handcrafted warmth (later runs).
- **Light only from real sources.** The sun through the windows, the sky through them, and the lamps; no "lid lifted off" sun. Closing the blinds or switching off the lights darkens the room, and changing the lights at 10 cm is a puzzle. See "Light from real sources" below.
- **Camera angles that read as art.** High-angle and isometric views with a tilt-shift miniature feel; every view gently blurs the foreground and the background, keeping the player and companion crisp. See "Cameras and depth of field" below.
- **Architecture styles are later player options**: medieval and cottage (`Tiny Glade 1.png`), urban (`Titl Shift 2.png`, `Titl Shift 3.png`), modern and sci-fi (`Scifi artistic.png`), and, from the 7 October frames, a classical palace in winter colours (`Tiny Glade 8.png`) and a Tudor village (`Tiny Glade 5.png`).
- **Avatars are felt figurines**, soft and pliable (a later packet; three options each for head, torso, arms and legs, mostly colour variations).

## The target in one paragraph

A real room, photographed by the player, that looks like a **handmade toy diorama on a tabletop**, seen by a 10 cm person. The founder's notes set the qualities: Tiny Glade's art style as the target; soft, slightly wonky, rounded forms instead of sharp sterile ones; subtle tactile texture on wood, brick and fabric so the world feels made by hand; warm, dynamic light with bounce, warm ambient and rich shadow contrast that shifts with the time of day; a palette that follows the real calendar (a muted winter, a vibrant spring and autumn, a verdant summer) and the real clock (darker, bluer nights with deeper shadows, warm pastel twilights); a depth of field like tilt-shift photography, where everything in reach stays crisp, far things soften, and anything between the camera and the character blurs away so you can see through it; colour that goes where the eye should go; and details that suggest someone lives there. Detail should appear as the camera comes closer rather than everywhere at once.

## Scoring rubric

A reviewer scores each criterion 0 to 10 from the review cameras, writes at least one specific critique per criterion (what is wrong, where, compared with which reference), and names the change that would raise it. The overall score is the weighted mean. The target is **8.5**, the same bar as the earlier art work. A score is evidence for the founder, not a pass.

| Criterion | Weight | Judge it on | 3: blockout | 6: on the way | 8.5: target | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Palette and desaturation | 1.0 | Ceiling corner, low corner, isometric room | One hue family everywhere, or raw saturated primaries | Calm saturation, but large areas of one beige or brown | Gently desaturated where it should be; warm and cool families both present; accents (rug, book, avatars) sing without shouting; the season reads | Could be mistaken for a Tiny Glade frame's palette |
| Warm key against cool shadow | 1.2 | All | Flat light; shade is grey or brown | Key and shade differ in value but both are warm | Sunlit planes clearly warm; shade and cast shadows clearly cool and never black; bounce warms surfaces near lit floor | Light alone tells the hour |
| Edge softness and form | 0.8 | Over the shoulder, companion, player eye | Hard CG edges, perfect boxes | Edges catch light but read machined | Box props read as rounded, slightly worn, hand-cut objects; no razor silhouettes at play distance | Every silhouette feels sculpted |
| Material handmade-ness per role | 1.2 | Over the shoulder, companion, player eye | Flat colour or photo noise | Visible marks that read as noise or as the wrong material | Each role reads as itself and as hand-made (see the table below): painted plaster, planks, woven rug, book pages, taped carton, rubber | A viewer wants to touch it |
| Clutter density | 0.6 | Ceiling corner, low corner | Empty box | Props spaced like a showroom | A lived-in diorama: small things in reach, quiet areas around paths | Tiny Glade 6 levels of intent |

## What "handmade" means per material role

Run 1 implements these in `game/shaders/painterly_material.gdshader` through `MaterialLibrary`, with the per-role numbers in the preset's `materials.roles` and the role's pattern, brush marks and calm in `x_look_role_marks`. All marks live in object space (they travel with an object) and fade out before they alias, so finer marks appear only as the camera comes close. These treatments belong to the style, not to a medium; a medium (felt, clay, cardboard) will later replace or retexture them.

| Role | Target | Run 1 treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `painted_wall`, `plaster`, `wallpaper` | Hand-rolled paint over plaster: soft clouds of value and temperature, faint roller direction, never a photo texture | Domain-warped broad washes and soft strokes, warm where light and cool where dark; light stroke normals; **calm 0.3** |
| `wood`, `wood_painted` | Planks laid by hand: visible seams and butt joints, each plank its own tone, grain that follows the long side, end grain different from face grain | Planks along an object's longest side, per-plank tone, staggered joints, ring streaks, rounded worn edges; **calm 0.28** (0.15 painted) |
| `fabric`, `carpet` | Woven, soft, slightly fuzzy; a rug has a border | Fine weave that fades with distance, sheen at grazing angles, a border band in the palette accent on rugs; no calm: accents carry colour |
| `paper` | A book or stack: cover boards and cream page edges | Cream page edges with fine page lines on three sides, covers top, bottom and spine |
| `cardboard` | A taped carton: flaps meeting in a seam, packing tape over it, fluting on the sides, scuffed corners | Seam and tape across the top and down two sides, faint flutes, heavy edge wear |
| `rubber` | Matte, dense, flecked | Sparse round flecks, high roughness |
| `metal`, `metal_painted` | Brushed or painted metal, worn bright at edges | Brushed streaks along the long side, low softening |
| `plastic`, `ceramic`, `glass`, `leather` | Smooth, a little glaze, soft highlights | Smooth with gentle mottling and a stronger highlight |
| `stone`, `concrete`, `brick` | Pitted, mottled, model-making plaster | Pores and specks; concrete may add the painted ground texture; **calm 0.25 to 0.3** |

Every role also gets: a glaze toward its tint where the preset gives one (hue only, the room's base colour keeps its lightness; the glaze is 0.12, so a captured or hand-picked palette reads as itself), albedo softening toward a gentle pastel, stroke normals, and, on box props, shading-only rounded edges with lighter worn paint (real bevels and wobble are L4, Run 2). **Calm** draws the role's colour toward its own grey after all that, so the room's big quiet surfaces sit back and the small accents carry the colour. Lighting on these materials uses a wrapped diffuse with a narrow warm band at the terminator, scaled by the light's own warmth, so the sun and lamps get it and the cool sky fill and the moon do not. Nothing adds light inside a cast shadow: shade is lit by the window's fill and the bounce.

**The landscape's roles** (Run 2: the land in the game). A landscape package's GLBs carry one primitive per harness role, the glTF material named for it, with `extras` that say `colors_baked` and its role, and COLOR_0 holding the tinted blends (grass to soil, rock to snow) over the material's colour envelope. The room's own treatment kept only the material's albedo, which for a blended role is the envelope's pale cream, and threw the vertex colour away: the ground showed cream where the generator meant meadow. A material whose extras say `colors_baked` now takes `painterly_land.gdshader`, which **keeps the vertex colour inside the painterly treatment** and gives each of the harness's twenty roles its own marks (`LandscapeLook.Roles`; every role of `pipeline/landscape/harness/common.py`'s palette, pinned by a test). The marks move the colour only along the harness's own three-colour ramp (darker and cooler, the colour, lighter and warmer), never away from it, and fade out before a pixel covers half a mark, so the broad washes carry the far hills and the fine marks appear as the 10 cm eye comes close. Terrain and merged plants are in world space and share their marks across parts; a prop (a cottage, the crate) has its own seed.

| Role | Target | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `meadow` | Combed grass in patches that turn yellow-green or blue-green | Blades along a wind axis, soft lighter tufts, rare flecks of seed head and flower on gentle ground; cooled a little by a glaze so the summer grade keeps it green |
| `soil`, `worn_path` | Turned earth; packed, trodden earth | Clods and pits; fine cross-hatching and the odd small stone |
| `gravel`, `scree`, `rock` | Pebbles; broken chips; model-making stone | Pebble cells each with its own tone and dark gaps; larger flat chips; broad facets of different temperature, a few hairline cracks and a hint of strata |
| `cliff` | A cut face | Wavy strata of alternating tone with dark seams and weathering streaks running down, none on the tops |
| `moss`, `snow` | Cushions; drifts | Soft mounds with bright tips; smooth, bluish in the hollows, a glitter of crystals |
| `still_water`, `flowing_water` | Painted water you can see into, no refraction (Run 2) | A glaze over the bed (`painterly_water.gdshader`, `WaterLook`): the eye's path through the water sets how much it hides, so the shallows are clear and the deep middle near opaque; the depth straight down to the bed sets the colour in a few soft washes with brushed edges, the generator's water colour lifted toward clear green in the shallows and darker and cooler in the deep; a pale wet line at the shore; ripples that drift (streaks running downhill for flowing water), a sky-coloured rim where it reflects most at grazing angles, a glint of the sun. Two-sided: from below it is a pale rippled ceiling, mirror-like toward grazing angles. It reads only the depth buffer (no screen copy) and casts no shadow of its own |
| `foliage`, `bark` | Leaf clusters; trunks | Rounded dabs of leaf paint, lighter toward the sky, darker underneath; furrows running up the trunk |

| `cloth`, `metal`, `stone` | Props | Woven threads; brushed streaks; pores |

**Captured meshes** (a minor of the Lane L review) get the same treatment: a mesh that arrives with plain glTF materials gets the painterly material of its role (a shell part's own role, or the asset slot named like the surface's material, else the asset's first role), keeping its own colour and colour texture, which the shader reads a few mip levels down (`x_look_paint.photo_blur_lod`) so photo noise and sensor grain go and what stays reads as a painter's block-in. The mesh keeps its own glTF materials underneath, and the GI bake voxelizes those, so the bounce still has the capture's real colours.

## Light from real sources

The founder (6 October): light comes only from real sources. The test room has a west window (the founder chose west for sunset light; the room's -Z is north), and the look lights it like this. Three shadowed lights in all: the sun, the window's sky fill and the ceiling lamp (`budgets.max_shadowed_lights` 3).

- **The sun, only through an opening.** The key is the real sun (`x_look_key_mode` `sun`) on a solar model, and the room's shell casts its shadows like everything else, so direct sun lands only where an opening lets it through: a gold patch that crosses the floor through the afternoon, with long shadows behind every prop and both avatars (`Tiny Glade 1.png`'s light). **A room with a `sun` light hint gets the sun at that hint's strength; a room with a window but no sun hint (the usual shape of a captured room) gets the sun at full strength through that window** (review M1); a room with neither is a closed box lit by its lamps.
- **The shell casts double-sided shadows** (review M4). A photographed room's walls are single surfaces, and a single-sided wall casts no shadow on the side it faces away from, so the sun used to pass through it (measured: with the sun switched on, a room with one single-sided wall and no window lit its floor by 0.45 in luma, with the shadows of the props lying on it; now 0.00). A closed slab loses nothing.
- **An opening must be a hole.** `RoomBuilder` does not yet cut `shell.openings` out of a solid wall, so a captured room's window is a painted square, the sun and the sky fill are blocked, and the room is black by day (the contract's garage example read 0.055 in mean luma at noon; the test room 0.47). The change is in `docs/look/proposals/roombuilder-openings.diff` (Lane P's files); with it a captured-like room (a solid wall with the window only listed, no sun hint) reads the same as the test room (0.381 against 0.379).
- **The sky through the window.** Each window light hint becomes a soft sky fill standing `window_standoff_m` (0.6 m) outside the opening and `window_light_size_m` (0.6 m) across, aimed in along the hint's direction, in the sky colour of the hour at `sky_fill_saturation` (0.55) of its hue, its brightness following the sky's. **The shadow budget goes to the brightest windows first, then the lamps; a fill that gets no shadow stands inside the room at its opening (2 cm inside the bounds), never behind the wall, where it would shine through it** (review M3).
- **What a window shows is the sky** (review M2). The environment's background is a painterly sky shader (`painterly_window_sky.gdshader`) driven by the hour, the season and the sun's and moon's real directions: a hazy horizon fading to the zenith, soft rounded clouds that catch the colour of the low sun, the sun's disc and halo by day, the moon and stars by night, and a meadow under the horizon in the season's colour. Before this a window was a dark slate square (`#2a2f33`, graded to `#383e47`) at noon, at sunset and at night alike. The sky lights nothing (ambient and reflections stay off), so it never changes the room's exposure; it is the brightest thing a window shows, so it glows, and its halo is the **golden window light** of the late afternoon.
- **A closed interior, and a volume that stops at the shell.** Floor, walls and ceiling are baked into VoxelGI as an interior, so what comes through the window bounces round the room (VoxelGI energy 1.0, propagation 0.6). The volume reaches only `x_look_gi.margin_m` (5 cm) past the room. At 30 cm it took in the slabs' outer faces, which the sun lights, and VoxelGI's cones carried that light through the wall: with the sun on and no way in, a closed room brightened by 0.10 (the test room at noon) to 0.44 (the garage example) in floor luma. At 5 cm it is 0.00 to 0.02, and the sunlit patch itself loses about a tenth. Moonlight leaked the same way, so the night sky was lifted a quarter. Inside an interior VoxelGI volume the environment's ambient light has no effect (measured: identical frames at ambient 0 and 1); `x_look_gi.environment_ambient_scale` (0.15) only matters where VoxelGI is missing, such as a renderer fallback.
- **Lamps are lamps,** warm white and a visible bulb. A lamp shines at one constant brightness when it is on. Until room state carries switches, lamps switch themselves on when daylight falls below `switch_on_below_daylight` (0.25, around sunset); `LookDirector.SetLamps(true/false/null)`, `--look-lamps=on|off|auto` or `GARAGE_GAME_LOOK_LAMPS` pin them. The style warms captured lamp colours 40% of the way toward `lamp_tint` (`#ffe3b8`, a warm white; it was orange). A lit lamp also shows its bulb, a small emissive sphere (`glow_radius_m`, `glow_energy`): a warm-white point of light that blooms near the camera and melts into a creamy disc in the blurred distance. The review frames pin the lamps on (the late afternoon of a lived-in room: sun through the window and a lamp lit).
- **Nights at moonlight level.** The founder: dark, but you can make out shapes. The moon stands 35 degrees up outside the room's brightest window, so cool moonlight falls in through it; it takes over from the sun only during civil twilight (the sun between 0 and -6 degrees), while the light is dim. With the lamps off at 02:00 the room is dark blue with every shape readable (frame mean luma 0.11 to 0.14); with the lamps on it is warm. The night grade (bluer, calmer, slightly deeper) applies in full under moonlight and only `night_with_lamps` (10%) under lamplight, so lamplight keeps its warmth.
- **Shadows that ground 10 cm bodies.** The sun's shadow is filtered (`shadow_blur` 1.55 before the season's own factor), not softened by PCSS: at the old 2.4 degrees of angular size PCSS erased the shadows of the avatars and the props entirely, and cost about 2 ms a frame. Biases are small and separate for the sun (`key_bias` 0.02, `key_normal_bias` 0.6) and the positional lights (`lamp_bias` 0.02, `lamp_normal_bias` 0.6); zero bias showed acne on the rug. The sun's shadow covers 1.1 room diagonals in two splits, the first over 30% of it. **The season sets the shadow's edge** (`x_look_seasons.looks.<season>.sun_blur`): crisp in summer, soft in winter.
- **Blinds and switches** (later): an opening's `state` and a lamp's on/off become room state changed through commands, and the look follows them. Operating them at 10 cm is Play-lane work.
- **Open land: a real sky lights the land** (Run 2; the founder, 8 October: the window only tells where the sun rises). "The sun lands only through the window" is an indoor rule. A room whose shell has no wall and no ceiling is **open land** (`LandscapeLook.IsOpenLand`; a landscape export's shell is ground and backdrop). There the sun needs no opening (it follows the solar model from the room's `site` as ever, and a landscape without a sun hint still gets it), the hour's ambient colour fills the shade (`OpenLandLight.AmbientScale`, 0.85 where a closed interior keeps 0.15), the sun is `OpenLandLight.SunStrength` (0.38) of the room look's (that sun is bright enough for one patch of floor in a dark room, energy 3.3 on a summer morning, and blew every lit surface of the land out to lime and cream), its shadows reach 30 m (the hills of the backdrop, not just a room), and no VoxelGI is baked (it bakes a closed interior; the land is open to the sky on every side, and the bake took 2.2 s and about 1.5 ms of frame for little bounce). Rooms with walls are unchanged. The numbers are in code (`OpenLandLight`) because v1 is locked; a later preset version moves them into the preset.
- **The Gubble's glow is a real light too** (Run 2, Glow): an omni light with a soft halo on the Gubble, or a small wisp at a point, in its aura colour or a warm white-gold, inside the light and shadow budgets. `LookDirector.LightLevelAt` estimates how lit a spot is, and `DarkThreshold` (0.5) says when the game counts it as dark. See [GLOW.md](GLOW.md).

## Time and season

- **The sun follows a solar model.** Its elevation and compass bearing come from the latitude, the date and the local standard clock time (NOAA's declination and equation-of-time series; sunrise and sunset where the sun's centre crosses -0.833 degrees, the almanac's definition), so the day is as long as it really is on every date. **Where the room stands is the room's own `site`** (review M5): the latitude (whole degrees), which way its -Z axis faces (the compass bearing, whole degrees) and the clock hour of mean solar noon (a quarter hour) in an optional `site` key of the manifest (`docs/look/proposals/room-manifest-site.diff` is the contract change). A site is deliberately coarse, with no longitude, place or finer latitude, and the loader refuses all of them. `StylePreset.WithSite` puts a room's site into the sun's tuning, so the shared preset carries none (a preset that names `latitude_deg` in `x_look_sun` is refused), and a room that declares none gets the look's fallback (30 degrees north, -Z facing north, solar noon at 12:00; the log says so, and `LookDirector.SiteNote` carries it). A room south of the equator has the seasons turned round. At 30 degrees north a day lasts 10.2 h at the December solstice, 12.1 h at the equinoxes and 14.1 h at the June solstice.
- **The real clock by default, read as standard time.** Time of day follows the computer's clock and seasons its calendar. While the machine's time zone is in daylight saving the clock is read an hour earlier (`real_clock_daylight_saving`), so the sun stays on standard time. The look re-reads the clock every 10 seconds; a moment whose grade is not cached builds it off the main thread. For reviews, playtests and tests the clock is deterministic and always standard time: review captures pin it from `tools/look/review_cameras.json` (**17:00 on 15 April**), code pins it with `LookDirector.SetClock`, and a player or reviewer can pin it with `--look-clock=HH:MM` and `--look-date=YYYY-MM-DD` after `--`, or `GARAGE_GAME_LOOK_CLOCK` and `GARAGE_GAME_LOOK_DATE`. `LookDirector.ClockNote` says which applies.
- **An island's own clock replaces the machine's.** A room state whose `extensions.x_clock` holds a clock record (zone, `day_scale`, seasons, phase, hemisphere; M6) is read through `WorldTime` in place of the machine's clock: the kernel keeps it (`CommandHost.ClockRecord`), the boot hands it to `LookDirector.SetWorldClockFrom`, and the HUD's time and season keys step from `LookDirector.RealNow`. With no record, or one the schema's checks refuse, the look follows the machine's clock as above. The island save does not carry the record yet, so today it comes only from an imported room state; Live will keep it.
- **Why 17:00 on 15 April.** At the earlier review moment (16:30 on 6 October) the sun stood 14 degrees up at bearing 256 and landed north of the spawns, so the avatars never caught it. At 17:00 on 15 April it stands 18 degrees up at 271 (due west), the patch crosses both spawns, and the avatars are lit from the side in golden light: a spring evening, so the vibrant spring palette is what the frames show.
- **The time keys colour the day.** The keys describe one reference day (its sunrise and sunset are where their daylight crosses `reference_daylight`); the real sunrise and sunset are mapped onto them, linearly through the day and the night, so the keys' gold afternoon and orange sunset always fall just before the real sunset, whatever the season. Through the golden hour the sun is gold and turns orange only near sunset; the sky colours are light blue, not lavender.
- **The seasons, stronger** (7 October). Each season has a grade (`seasons.grades`: saturation, warmth and a tint) and a look (`x_look_seasons.looks`: the sun's strength, the softness of its shadow edge, the sky's saturation and brightness, and its cloud). They are held around the season's middle and cross-faded between: **winter** muted (saturation 0.55, a cool tint, a weaker sun at 0.7, soft shadows, a pale cloudy sky), **spring** vibrant (1.4, a clear sky with some cloud), **summer** verdant (1.2, the sun at 1.25 with the crispest shadow edge and a clear deep-blue sky), **autumn** vibrant (1.3, a warm tint, a golden sun). The palette's own saturation is 0.9, so a strong colour keeps about half its chroma in winter and gains a quarter in spring. The grade's cast stays gentle (the founder, 6 October: the room's greens, blues, creams and pinks read as themselves); the seasons show in saturation, sunlight and sky, not in a tint over everything.
- **The seasons on the land** (Run 2, L10): the leaves, the meadow and the ground follow the season the grade follows (broadleaves fresh and some in blossom in spring, gold to rust tree by tree in autumn, pale under a dusting of snow in winter; conifers green; grass to straw and frost); albedo only, the light never reads it. See [SEASONS.md](SEASONS.md).
- **Grade.** After tone mapping, a 3D LUT applies warmth, the season tint, cool shadows and nearly neutral highlights, the palette saturation, night, and contrast. LUTs are cached by their (quantized) inputs, and a moment the cache has not seen builds off the main thread.
- **What the look test checks.** At every minute of four days, one per season: light changes smoothly and rises once from its darkest minute (at night) to its brightest and falls once back; the key comes from the real sun's direction whenever the sun is up and moves only as fast as the sun does while bright; it swings to the moon only while dim and never shines from below the horizon. The solar model: day lengths on the solstices and equinoxes, through the year, noon elevations, sunrise bearings, other latitudes, and the standard-time reading of the clock. The room site: its reader and what it refuses, the fallback and its note, and the sun worked out for a room's own latitude, facing and hemisphere. Real sources: physics rays from the built room show direct sun reaching the floor only through the window at the review moment and on a summer evening; every shell part casts the sun's shadow from both sides; nights at moonlight level with the lamps' switch; a cool light-blue fill and a warm sun through the golden hour; and the grade tints the room's own colours by less than a just-noticeable step (0.015 in Oklab). The light protections a mutation once removed unnoticed are pinned: the clock follows the real one (a faked clock in the test) and is re-read only at its interval, the lamps switch and pin, a room with a window gets the sun, a third window's fill never stands outside the shell.
- **Every look number is in the preset.** Beyond the schema fields, the preset's `x_look_*` blocks hold every number the look uses. The code keeps defaults only for presets written before these blocks; the look test fails if the shipped preset leaves any number to them. The blocks are described by a generated typed schema (`tools/look/make_look_schema.py`; proposal `style-preset-look-tuning.diff`), so the contract can check them (review M6). `renderer.shadows.tint` is read but not used while light comes only from real sources: shade takes the colour of the light that reaches it.
- **Renderer fallback.** When the machine renders with something other than the preset's renderer (for example Compatibility after a Vulkan failure), the look logs a `LOOK WARNING`, the review harness records it and fails the capture, and `LookDirector.PlayerNotice` carries a sentence for the HUD. It is never silent.

## Cameras and depth of field

The founder's direction: the player chooses over-the-shoulder, first person, or an **isometric or high-angle view** that shows more of the world around the character, and **every view gently blurs the foreground and background**. While moving, focus follows the player and the companion; while building and zoomed out, focus follows the mouse or a free camera the player controls.

**13 October, the two views (decided by the founder):** the game commits to first person (F1) and the over-the-shoulder view (F2), which zooms out with the wheel from its shoulder arm to 8 m and, past 0.8 m, orbits the player with movement following the camera; the diorama (F3) and isometric (F4) views are shelved in play, their keys unbound, and `hud_view` 2 and 3 remain for this harness only (`observe_f3`, `observe_f4`). The sea's seam moved out with the camera's reach (`OpenSea.CameraReachM`, 8.5 m): about a minute and a half's swim, 16.5 m past the island.

- **Focus follows the avatars.** Focus sits on the player's body. When the companion is within `x_look_dof.companion_follow_m` (0.8 m) of the player, the crisp band stretches to keep it crisp too, so the two read as one subject. From the player's own eye the focus sits just beyond reach and the reach (`eye_crisp_body_heights`, 1.5 body heights) stays crisp; anything nearer than 8 cm blurs.
- **Focus eases.** It used to snap when the focus point jumped (the player stepped, the cursor crossed the room). The band now moves toward the wanted one with a time constant of `focus_ease_s` (0.12 s), like an eye refocusing. A framed review camera takes its band at once.
- **Focus follows the cursor while building.** `LookDirector.FocusOverride` takes a world point (the build camera's cursor hit or a free camera's target); while it is set, every unframed camera focuses there. The preset's `"cursor"` focus mode uses it too.
- **Tilt-shift from above.** Looking down on the room narrows the crisp band (`tilt_band_narrowing`), shortens the blur ramps (`tilt_transition_shortening`) and strengthens the blur (`tilt_amount_gain`), scaled by `camera.tilt_shift.strength` (0.8). From 45 degrees up the crisp band is about 30 cm deep around the avatars, the floor at the bottom of the frame sits in front of it and the far room at the top is deep in the blur: the tabletop-miniature read. The player's level eye view is unchanged.
- **The observe view** (7 October; refocused in Run 2). `LookDirector.Observe` switches to a very tight profile for looking at what was built with the companion (`Titl Shift 3.png`): a crisp band `observe_band_m` deep around the focus point, short ramps (10 cm far, 8 cm near) and full blur beyond. It does not stretch for the companion. **Focus (Run 2, brief 07):** in a third-person view (F1's follow camera, F3, F4) the focus is the player's rendered body (the interpolated middle of the 10 cm figure), ahead of any point set from outside; the HUD no longer hands the look the F3/F4 orbit pivot, which sat between the two avatars and left both soft in the founder's third playtest, and it moves its camera before the look reads it (process priority -1). The player's own eye camera keeps its rule. In observe the band follows the camera's depth at once, with no ease, so a view swap or a step never leaves the figure out of the band for a moment; ordinary focus still eases. A close view whose arm a wall shortens keeps the near edge inside its own focus. **The band is v1's 6 cm and v2's 14 cm:** a 10 cm body seen from 20 to 80 degrees up needs up to 5.6 cm of depth each side of its middle (half the height and the radius, with the seating gap), so v1's 6 cm could not hold the whole body; v2's 14 cm does at every pitch the F3 view allows and at F4's. **The blur amount is v1's 0.3 and v2's 0.2:** see Budgets. The switch is the HUD's key, **O**. The `observe_view`, `observe_f3` and `observe_f4` review cameras show it.
- **Colour contrast as focus** (the focus pass). The grain and vignette effect also reads the depth buffer and works out, for every pixel, how far it is outside the depth-of-field band the look gave the camera (the same distances, ramps and near blur). Inside the band colour is lifted (`focus_saturation` 1.18); outside it is drawn back (`defocus_saturation` 0.72), so the figures in focus carry the colour and the blurred distance sits back. It runs on the linear colour before the depth-of-field blur, which is what lets the next point work.
- **Creamy bokeh** (the focus pass again, and the project's circular bokeh kernel, `rendering/camera/depth_of_field/depth_of_field_bokeh_shape` 2). Highlights in the blurred region are lifted (`bokeh_gain` 1.2 above a luma of `bokeh_threshold` 1) before the blur spreads them, so a bright window, a lamp bulb or a sunlit patch melts into a soft bright disc, as in `Tilt Shift 6` and `Titl Shift 3.png`, instead of vanishing into the blur.
- **Finish.** A static, paper-like grain and a light vignette (`post.grain`, `post.vignette`) finish the frame.
- **Anti-aliasing is FXAA** (the integrator's change from TAA, which blurred the running body). Whether floorboards shimmer under it is measured, not assumed: `capture-look.ps1 -Probe shimmer` glides a camera sideways over the floor in sub-pixel steps and compares each frame with a supersampled reference of the same pose. See `docs/look/reviews/run1/CAPTURES.md`.

## Living scenery: fish (Run 2)

The founder (8 October): "Can there be fish in the water?", answered as living scenery: small schools that swim around the ponds and dart away when you come close, no catching yet, bigger ponds get more fish. The code is `game/scripts/native/Look/Fauna/`; the numbers are starting points for the founder's eye, not rules.

- **Where.** `PondSurvey` finds the room's water (shell parts of material `water`), splits it into bodies, and finds the bed under each 1.5 cm cell by a ray down to the world layer. A school's home needs water at least 4.5 cm deep (the brief's 6 cm, lowered so today's 5.6 cm garage tarn holds fish); fish swim anywhere at least 3 cm deep and keep to the upper 9 cm of deeper water. One school per 0.15 m² of home water, at most six a pond and 72 fish a room; a pond of a square metre or more with a deep middle gets one big fish. A shallow brook gets none, and so does water with no bed under it (the far lake).
- **What.** Painted fish 1.5 to 3 cm long (the avatar is 10 cm): olive minnows with a dark stripe along the flank, golden fish with pale blotches, and the odd bronze carp of about 4.5 cm with a hint of scales; a darker, cooler back, a pale belly, a dark eye, paler fins with rays, a tail that beats in a travelling wave. One MultiMesh, one draw, under 200 triangles a fish.
- **How they move.** A small boids model per school: loosely together (no pull within 3.5 cm of the school's middle), swimming the same way, two body lengths apart, wandering toward a new goal every few seconds, turning where the water shallows ahead, between bed and surface. They cruise at up to 5 cm/s. An avatar in or at the water (its feet no more than 7 cm above the surface) within 16 cm startles the fish near it: they dart away at up to 32 cm/s, the whole school takes fright, and they settle back over about 1.6 s and regroup within a few more.
- **Rules.** Placement is deterministic from the room's id and its water; the motion runs freely (and is deterministic for the same steps, which the tests use). Fish are not entities: not saved, not in the kernel, sight or map, and they collide with nothing.
- **Under the water.** When a camera dips below a pond's surface, a full-screen veil clouds the water with distance (two thirds hidden at 22 cm) and stops at the surface, so the underside stays clear; above water its vertex stage draws nothing.

**Fractals** (Run 2, L9): the found things that grow the body, drawn where the generator placed them as a small star of light that bobs inside the kernel's box, with a glint that reads from a band away; at night each casts a small unshadowed pool of light on the ground around it (the founder: "a fun way for players to find it"; at most 32 lit, those nearest the player), and its pickup sends up a few sparks. See [FRACTALS.md](FRACTALS.md).

**The hamlet's charm** (Run 2, L11): the life details of a lived-in place hung on the generator's hamlet: smoke from the chimneys (thicker once the lamps are on), warm windows and a lantern's glow at night, bunting and washing on strings between the cottages swaying in the meadow's wind, and fairy lights at night; it lights nothing, collides with nothing and is never saved. ## Budgets

The preset budget is **16.7 ms per frame at 1920 × 1080 on an NVIDIA RTX 2070 SUPER (8 GB)**, at most three shadowed lights (sun, window sky fill, lamp). Reviews report p50, p95 and p99 frame time per camera; the numbers of this round are in `docs/look/reviews/run1/CAPTURES.md`. The VoxelGI bake runs once at room load (under 0.5 s for the test room) and is not part of the frame budget.

**The observe view's frame budget (Run 2).** Run 1 measured `observe_view` at 17.34 ms p95 against 16.7, the one frame over. The cost is one number. Godot's circular bokeh (`depth_of_field_bokeh_shape` 2) runs at half resolution and, at every pixel whatever the frame holds, gathers about (64 x amount)^2 / (2 x blur_scale) samples (`bokeh_dof.glsl`; blur_scale is 1.0 at the project's bokeh quality 2), so the observe profile costs over the ordinary tilt-shift in proportion to its blur amount **squared**: the band and the ramps do not move it. Run 1: `diorama_high` (amount 0.165) 10.07 ms of GPU time, `observe_view` (0.3) 11.98 ms, which is 30.4 ms per unit of amount squared. v2 sets `observe_amount` to **0.2**: about 82 samples a pixel instead of 184, 1.5 ms less on those numbers, p95 15.8 ms by the model (a check in the look test pins it at least 0.5 ms inside the budget). Rendered on this round's machine (`docs/look/reviews/run2/CAPTURES.md`), p95 fell from 13.96 to 13.10 ms in `observe_view`, from 14.45 to 13.33 in `observe_f3` and from 13.99 to 13.68 in `observe_f4` while the unchanged cameras ran 4% slower, and against `diorama_high` of the same run the observe view's p95 fell from 1.126 to 1.027 times, which on Run 1's `diorama_high` (14.91 ms) is 16.8 before (Run 1 measured 17.3) and 15.3 after. The trade is a blur radius a third smaller (38 px to 26 px at 1080p). The lever that would keep 0.3 is the project's bokeh quality (`rendering/camera/depth_of_field/depth_of_field_bokeh_quality`, 2 now, the integrator's file): quality 1 uses blur_scale 4, four times fewer samples everywhere, at the price of a grainier bokeh.

At full mist the swimmer's water is clear to 2 m and everything past 8 m from the camera is wholly mist, so from the seam out no play camera sees any of the island. On the island garage the seam is 17.6 m from the centre, 65 s of swimming past the reef. Lane P's wrap goes beyond it; `OpenSea.SeamFor(sea, bounds)` gives it from room data alone.
- **Architecture kits and density**: consistent themes (palace, cantina, modern city, Tudor village), dense layered buildings (`Tiny Glade 6.png`). Each theme keeps one palette, one material family and one scale of detail.
- **The life details of a lived-in place** (laundry, bunting, planters, fences, benches, streetlights, chimney smoke, a swing or wagon that implies someone off screen) and fairy lights: L5 procedural charm. The preset's `charm` block is reserved for it and stays disabled.
- **Material media** (felt, clay, yarn, cardboard and so on): deferred by the founder until the style is settled; each needs research into real handcrafted work in that medium.
- **L4 handmade geometry**: real bevels, slight wobble and softened silhouettes on meshes. Run 1 fakes rounded edges in shading only.
- **L6 offline restyle** of captured assets' textures (the runtime now only paints them).
- **Felt figurine avatars** (later packet).
- **Kuwahara and outline post effects**: in the schema, disabled in the preset, not implemented.
- **Blinds and switches** as room state (above).
- **A Compatibility fallback**: none; add one only if a measured device requirement appears.
